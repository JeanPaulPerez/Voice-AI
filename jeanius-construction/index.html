01 / Tear-off & assessment

We don't roof over problems.

Layering new shingles over old ones hides rot and voids most manufacturer warranties. We strip every course to the deck, pull the old nails, and haul it all away the same day. Restoration candidates get the same honesty: if 80% of your roof is sound, we tell you, and we fix the 20%.

RemovedOld 3-tab asphalt · granule loss · cupping

02 / Decking

Rotten wood gets replaced, not covered.

Every sheet is sounded and walked. Soft, delaminated, or stained decking comes out and gets replaced with ⅝" CDX plywood, fastened 6" on-center at the edges. You approve each replacement sheet before it goes in, at the per-sheet price on your quote.

Spec⅝" CDX · 8d ring-shank · 6" O.C.

03 / Ice-and-water & underlayment

The layer you'll never see does the most work.

Self-adhering ice-and-water shield goes down first along every eave, 24" past the interior wall line as NJ and NY code requires, plus the rakes and around the chimney. Synthetic underlayment covers the rest of the deck as a second water barrier if a shingle ever lifts.

BarrierIce & water shield · eaves, rakes, penetrations

04 / Shingles

From the eave up. Staggered. Six nails a shingle.

Starter strip at the eaves and rakes, then architectural shingles in offset courses so no seam lines up with the one beneath it. Six nails per shingle in the nail line, not four, for a 130-mph wind rating. Hip-and-ridge caps seal the top, never cut-up field shingles.

FieldArchitectural asphalt · 130 mph · 6-nail

05 / Flashing, vents & gutters

Most leaks aren't the shingles. They're the details.

New step flashing and counter-flashing at the chimney, new pipe boots, drip edge on every edge, and balanced intake and exhaust ventilation so the attic runs cool and the shingles reach their rated life. Seamless aluminum gutters go on last, pitched to drain.

DetailsStep flashing · drip edge · ridge vent · seamless gutter

Insurance claims

How a storm-damage claim actually works.

Wind, hail, and fallen trees are usually covered. Age and neglect aren't. Here is the process, with us in it.

  1. Document within 48 hours

    We photograph and measure the damage, note the storm date, and write a report you can hand to your carrier. Tarping goes on the same visit if the roof is open.

  2. You file the claim

    You call your insurer, not us. You keep control of your policy. We give you the report and the language adjusters look for.

  3. We meet your adjuster on the roof

    We walk the roof with the adjuster so nothing gets missed: creased shingles, damaged vents, gutter dents, interior stains.

  4. Scope reconciled to code

    NJ and NY require ice-and-water shield and drip edge on a replacement. If the adjuster's scope leaves them off, we submit a code-upgrade supplement with the citations.

  5. Build, then release

    You pay your deductible, insurance pays the approved scope, and the recoverable depreciation gets released when we send the completion photos.

We never waive deductibles. A contractor who offers to "eat" your deductible is committing insurance fraud in both New Jersey and New York, and will make that money back somewhere on your roof.
