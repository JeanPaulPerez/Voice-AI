How it works

Start with a brief, not a search.

Portals show you everything so that you have to do the work. We ask you what you want first, then show you only what fits, including homes we hold that are not publicly marketed.

  1. 01

    Tell us what you need

    A short call or a note. Neighborhood, type of home, light, outdoor space, timing, and what you would rather avoid. Ten minutes is enough.

  2. 02

    We show you what fits

    A short list, not a feed. Where we hold homes that are not publicly marketed, you will see them here first, with floor plans and honest notes on each.

  3. 03

    View on your schedule

    Private appointments, evenings and weekends included. No open houses. If you are not in New York, we walk it with you on a live video call first.

  4. 04

    Negotiate and close

    Offer strategy, co-op board packages, attorneys, mansion tax and closing costs, handled with you rather than around you.

Before you ask

The questions buyers put to us first.

Is my search confidential?
Yes. We do not share your name, your budget or the homes you have seen with anyone outside the transaction, including other clients and other brokers, unless you ask us to.
What price range do you work in?
Residences from $750,000 upward. Put your range in the brief and we will tell you plainly whether we can help, and if not, who can.
Condo, cooperative or townhouse?
Each behaves differently. Co-ops ask for full financial disclosure and a board process that commonly runs 60 to 90 days, and many restrict pied-à-terre use. Condominiums close faster and suit international or part-time buyers. A townhouse gives you the whole building, and its upkeep. We will tell you which suits your situation before you fall for a particular one.
I am not in New York. Can I still view?
Yes. We do a first walk-through with you on a live video call, then hold the appointment for when you are here. We also work directly with your attorney, family office or assistant if you prefer.
What does it cost to enquire?
Nothing, and there is no obligation. If you go on to buy, plan for closing costs of roughly two to four percent of the purchase price, including New York's mansion tax on purchases of $1 million and above. You will have a line-by-line estimate before you make an offer.
